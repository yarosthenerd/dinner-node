0. Summary

DinnerNode is a testnet experiment. It routes text prompts to independently operated computers that run language models, and it pays those operators in valueless testnet MON, one micropayment roughly every two seconds.

  • Two things about you are written permanently to a public blockchain: a salted hash of your sanitized prompt, and your wallet address.
  • Your prompt text is not written to the chain. The node operator serving your job does see it in plaintext.
  • Outputs are machine generated by an AI system and may be wrong.
  • The hosted cloud kitchen does not run a model at all. It returns a fixed demo passage.
  • MON here has no monetary value. Nothing here is an investment or a payment service.
  • Do not enter personal data of any kind.

2. Data and privacy

2.1 What is written to the blockchain, permanently

When you open a job, exactly two items of data about you are recorded on the Monad testnet:

  • A salted keccak256 hash commitment of the sanitized prompt that opened the job. The browser generates a fresh random 32-byte salt for each job, prepends it to the sanitized prompt text, and hashes the result. The hash is passed to the contract as the promptTag field of openJob and emitted in the JobOpened event.
    One commitment per job, not per message. A conversation reuses one job across turns, and promptTag is computed once, when that job is opened. Later messages in the same conversation are sent to the node and answered, but nothing about them is written to the chain: no hash of them exists on chain, and the commitment recorded at openJob describes only the first message. The contract has no way to add a commitment to a job that is already open. Do not read the on-chain record as a per-message log; it is not one.
  • Your guest wallet address. It is the transaction sender, and it is also recorded as an indexed topic on the JobOpened event and on refunds and deposits.

Both are public forever. Anyone in the world can read them, at any time, without your permission and without notifying you. Blockchain records cannot be edited or removed by us or by anyone else.

2.2 What is not written to the blockchain

The text of your prompt is never written to the chain, and neither is the model's reply. Only the hash commitment described above is written, and only for the message that opened the job.

2.3 The node operator does see your prompt

The sanitized prompt is transmitted in plaintext to the node operator selected to serve your job, because a language model cannot answer a prompt it cannot read. The accurate description of the privacy position is this: the chain sees a hash and your wallet address, and the node operator sees the prompt.

2.4 The sanitizer is best-effort and is not a guarantee

Before your prompt is hashed or transmitted, the browser runs a local sanitization pass that attempts to replace common categories of personal data, including email addresses, phone numbers, IP addresses, URLs, street addresses, personal names, identity and passport numbers, payment card numbers, and API keys or secrets.

This pass is regular-expression pattern matching. It is best-effort only. It will miss personal data that does not match its patterns, it will miss personal data expressed in unusual formats or in languages other than English, and it can produce false matches that alter your prompt in ways you did not intend. It is not a guarantee, and you must not treat it as one.

2.5 Do not enter personal data

Do not enter personal data of any kind into DinnerNode. This includes your own personal data and, in particular, personal data about anyone else. Do not enter names, contact details, identifiers, location details, health information, financial information, credentials, confidential business information, or anything you would not be willing to publish. If you enter personal data anyway, you do so on your own responsibility, and the consequences described in section 2.6 apply to it.

2.6 Erasure, stated honestly

We cannot delete anything that has been written to the blockchain. No party can. If you make a request for erasure of on-chain data, we will not be able to satisfy it in the ordinary sense of deleting a record, and we will tell you so rather than pretend otherwise.

What we can describe is the design that limits what the on-chain record discloses. The per-job salt is 32 random bytes generated in your browser, used once to build the hash, and never stored anywhere: not on our servers, not in your browser, and not in the transaction. It ceases to exist when the page function that made it returns. Once the salt is gone, the on-chain hash cannot be checked against a candidate prompt by anyone, including us, because the value required to reproduce it no longer exists. That is what renders the hash functionally unlinkable to your prompt. This is a property of the construction rather than a legal guarantee of erasure, and it does not apply to your wallet address, which remains plainly readable on chain.

Your wallet address is a persistent public identifier. If you have ever associated that address with your identity anywhere, on this service or elsewhere, your use of DinnerNode is linkable to you. Use a fresh testnet address if this matters to you. This applies with more force when you connect your own wallet: an address you already use elsewhere carries whatever history and associations it already has, and every job you open from it joins that record. The throwaway wallet exists for exactly this reason and remains the default.

2.7 What is stored in your browser

The stores listed below live in your own browser. We hold no server-side copy of any of them, and their contents are not uploaded to us wholesale. That is not the same as saying nothing derived from them ever leaves your machine, and the difference matters:

  • You order from one of two wallets. By default it is a throwaway wallet whose private key is generated in your browser and held in dn_pk; that key never leaves your browser. If you connect your own browser wallet instead, we never see its key either, and we ask it to sign each transaction rather than signing for you.
  • The address of whichever wallet is active is written to the blockchain by every transaction you make. It is sent to our testnet faucet endpoint at /api/topup automatically when a throwaway wallet's balance runs low, and, for a connected wallet, only when you press the faucet control yourself. We do not top up a connected wallet without being asked.
  • Your sanitized prompt is sent to whichever node you select, in plaintext, and to no one else. The hosted cloud kitchen that used to receive it when a node failed has been removed, so there is no longer any fallback path that sends your prompt to infrastructure we run. If the node you selected fails, the order fails. Section 2.4 describes what sanitization does and does not remove.
  • If you select a behaviour template or paste a custom engram, its instruction text is prepended to the prompt and is sent to the node with it. It is written by you or chosen by you, so treat its content as you would treat the prompt itself.

3.1 What you are billed for

Nodes bill by output token at the rate each node publishes on its own /health endpoint. Your prompt is not billed. Output is.

Reasoning models produce two kinds of output: the reasoning they work through, and the visible answer. Both are billed as output tokens. On the reference node the reasoning is often larger than the answer, and on a long request it can be roughly three times the size, so the number of tokens you are charged for is usually well above the length of the reply you read. The reasoning is shown to you in the interface as it streams, so what you are charged for is visible rather than hidden. This is the same convention commercial inference providers use.

You are not billed for work that produced nothing. When you ask a node to plan a job, the planning is billed as output like anything else, but only if it produces a plan you can actually run: a planner that spends its budget and returns no valid plan costs the node its compute and costs you nothing. When a plan runs, each step is billed as it completes and delivers its output. A step that fails for a technical reason, or that is cut short because you stopped the run, is written off and never settled. Steps that already completed remain billed, because their output was delivered to you.

One consequence is worth stating plainly, because an earlier version of this page claimed more. When a node hands an unfinished answer to a replacement node, the replacement verifies a keccak checkpoint of the visible answer so far and bills only the tokens it writes itself. That check covers the visible answer only. Reasoning tokens are streamed and billed but are not part of the checkpoint chain, so a settlement cannot be reconstructed in full from the published text. The protection against being charged twice for the same visible answer is real and verifiable. It does not extend to reasoning.

4. Every answer comes from a real model

An earlier version of this section disclosed a hosted "cloud kitchen" endpoint that returned a fixed, pre-written passage while settling real testnet MON. That endpoint has been removed. It no longer exists at any address, and no request to this service is answered with pre-written text.

Every answer you receive now comes from a model running on a registered node. If the node you selected fails, the order fails and the escrow is returned; nothing substitutes for it.

Node operators run their own hardware and their own model. A node publishes which engine and model it runs on its /health endpoint, and the interface shows you that, so a node reporting a mock engine is visible to you before you order rather than disclosed here afterwards.
